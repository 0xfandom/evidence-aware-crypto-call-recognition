# Current Call Policy v2 — assessment summary

This newly written summary covers a BUY-recognition exercise. It does not replace the owner's full policy or change a scoring methodology.

## Three semantic outcomes

**BUY:** The captured author communicates a sufficiently identifiable asset and a present actionable entry recommendation, continuing opportunity endorsement or clear current acquisition intent. The recommendation can be implicit; no particular keyword is mandatory. A claimed action is not verified execution.

**NO_NEW_BUY:** Available context supports the absence of a new own BUY. This includes pure forecasts, past trades/profits/regret, HOLD and existing positions without a new entry, non-investment promotion, rejected/parodied/third-party calls, and genuine current SELL/short-only statements. This exercise collapses the non-BUY side for a specific purpose; it does not redefine SELL as informational or measure SELL recall.

**UNCLEAR:** An essential semantic referent, timing, attribution or context gap cannot be resolved. An irrelevant post, an unanswered open alternative, an imagined follower reaction or absent but nonessential media does not automatically create uncertainty.

## Boundaries to preserve

1. Evaluate the author's communication, not whether a hypothetical follower might trade.
2. A favorable price scenario or general optimism alone is insufficient. A supported own asset-specific present opportunity thesis may qualify even when it discusses uncertain future upside.
3. Past activity is separate from a new present clause. Do not discard a new entry merely because the post also describes an old exit; do not transfer historic profits or entry prices.
4. Existing holdings and a profitable short do not create a new recommendation. Selling holdings, opening a short, covering a short and initiating a long are distinct operations.
5. Future conditional action remains conditional unless the supplied source actually communicates a new current action. Do not invent an observed trigger or a price history.
6. Sponsorship is context: it neither proves nor disqualifies a genuine recommendation. Software purchases, account setup, free claims and referral mechanics are not automatic token BUYs.
7. Preserve negation, satire, imitation and quoted speakers. A context author's old call is not the primary author's current call. A primary author's explicit current renewal may be a new recommendation.
8. Questions can be genuine alternatives or rhetorical endorsements; punctuation alone does not decide.
9. Separate asset/action statements. A BUY of one asset does not imply a BUY of every mentioned asset. Use decision-specific evidence rather than making a full-post citation prove all actions.
10. Missing images, links and parents cannot be reconstructed. Sufficient primary text still supports a classification when those missing materials are nonessential.
11. Publication time does not verify trade time. An undated own purchase claim can remain unresolved.
12. Source identity, exact contract identity, execution proof, historical outcomes and score eligibility are separate layers. Missing chain/contract information does not negate a clear linguistic BUY.

## Technical evidence layer

Use exact supplied synthetic source IDs, author IDs, publication times, text hashes and request-local evidence references. Text/hash tampering, missing mandatory identity/time, future context and catalogue substitution are `INPUT_INVALID`, not `UNCLEAR`. A checksum proves byte consistency, not real-world authenticity. These fixtures simulate evidence contracts; no real account ownership or source verification is asserted.

Reference binding is necessary and insufficient: a technically valid quotation may support an incorrect interpretation. Give a concise factual explanation and preserve uncertainty. Never claim verified execution or production/scoring eligibility.
